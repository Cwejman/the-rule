## 4. The thesis: we no longer hold our own content

Directories and documents sit very close to our culture and history. Documents and files are a simple pattern at the heart of the operating system, for structuring knowledge and data. As tools grew and programs grew, the medium of the computer grew into cloud systems. We no longer hold our own content as sovereigns.

*The author's, 2026-10-04, dictated. "Anna's tools" is taken as "as tools".*

## 5. Our own data layer, which locks no one in or out

The first question for our work and our foundation is whether we ship a custom data layer. If we do, that is not a problem at all. A common substrate, such as wanting all your files in the Microsoft suite, means having everything in a monolith, in what we might call a bounded structural world. Something common may not be needed if what we have is a set of requirements on the data layer, and those requirements allow it to be joined to other data layers. The data layer is then made the way MCP is made for AI: we build the connection that stores our data in another data layer, as master or as slave.

Then we can ship our own and still give the very best way to lock no one in or out.

That is of course the foundation of OpenLight's ideas, made more concrete and real through the work of the rule.

*The author's, 2026-10-04, dictated.*

## 6. Storage that is good, with compute that comes and goes

Moving down to the layer things run on, what it runs on need not matter much. What matters is that there are always several parties. With the parties and the data layers left free, we can look at the old split of client and server. What is needed to run something? What is needed to own something? Local, through an installed application. Shared, through a remote that is reachable. Remote, hosted by a provider serving compute or serving storage. Or your own storage on compute. Really, it should be good storage with a layer of compute that comes and goes as needed.

*The author's, 2026-10-04, dictated.*
