## 5. Our own data layer, which locks no one in or out

The first question for our work and our foundation is whether we ship a custom data layer. If we do, that is not a problem at all. A common substrate, such as wanting all your files in the Microsoft suite, means having everything in a monolith, in what we might call a bounded structural world. Something common may not be needed if what we have is a set of requirements on the data layer, and those requirements allow it to be joined to other data layers. The data layer is then made the way MCP is made for AI: we build the connection that stores our data in another data layer, as master or as slave.

Then we can ship our own and still give the very best way to lock no one in or out.

That is of course the foundation of OpenLight's ideas, made more concrete and real through the work of the rule.

## 6. Storage that is good, with compute that comes and goes

Moving down to the layer things run on, what it runs on need not matter much. What matters is that there are always several parties. With the parties and the data layers left free, we can look at the old split of client and server. What is needed to run something? What is needed to own something? Local, through an installed application. Shared, through a remote that is reachable. Remote, hosted by a provider serving compute or serving storage. Or your own storage on compute. Really, it should be good storage with a layer of compute that comes and goes as needed.

## 14. Typed data replaces what programs are; the world's infrastructure took the files

We now know there are two things. There is typed data, whose purpose is very wide: essentially replacing what programs are today. Unix was singular in making the programs, and the pieces the OS itself was built on, first-class citizens of the unifying substrate. What happened in our evolution is that the ownership of directories and files moved more and more online, as online became interactive and stateful. Data was naturally cloudified and segregated; there are no longer files on your system. Structured data is naturally needed in programs, but the programs became monoliths, and the OS became a monolith, and even more so the monoliths of the web, which are now a matter of infrastructural ownership. It is not the infrastructure of your own environment, your own computer; it is the infrastructure of the world, an economic, political, or really just market-based separation of what could be unified.

## 16. The audience must be the owner

If I put focus on what is singularly important: one might think a rewrite of a traditional application today is as it used to be. I think it is not. The data an application rests on should be transparent and unifiable, because the audience of the product, or, with a less sellable name, the solution or program, must be the owner, since that is what compounds. So far we do not seem to create that space for people, their data and AI.

So if I build a new application, I will not make my own database, my own traditional back-end framework and some front end. I want to base it on a strong foundation for data and knowledge, and then use archetypally valuable navigation frameworks for it. Editability in these comes shipped.

So we are to create that foundation here, not as something to be superseded by your real product, but to be unified with the real product, integrable with ownership.
