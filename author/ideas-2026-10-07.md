## 3. Two numbers, writing and reading

Naturally then, save the effort I've spent. You know that from the number of prompts I've written and the approximate time of writing them. And there's a lot I do not read of the output you give me. Each prompt can say whether I've read, if we want to spend that compute, but perhaps that's not necessary. If it's simple to do, save two numbers: one in terms of time spent writing, the amount of writing; the second an approximation of how long I might have spent reading.

## 5. Read only where it is evidently used

We go the other way around: I generally read about 20% of what is said. So yes, we save the raw data, even in terms of character or word count. The only problem is that there isn't any intelligence in terms of what was plausibly read, so I would go the opposite way: only where it is obvious that the generated text is used in the reasoning is it read. Intelligence could be used further, but that is opaque data.

*The author's, 2026-10-07, answering whether reading is counted as a ceiling lowered where a prompt shows it unread.*

## 6. The changes are not counted as read

Don't include reading time in terms of the change made. The diffs contain the change made, and what of it was read is even harder to imply with confidence, so that might be running further ahead of the honest data we can have. But you could definitely mention these possibilities.
