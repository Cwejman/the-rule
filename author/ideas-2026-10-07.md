## 5. Read only where it is evidently used

We go the other way around: I generally read about 20% of what is said. So yes, we save the raw data, even in terms of character or word count. The only problem is that there isn't any intelligence in terms of what was plausibly read, so I would go the opposite way: only where it is obvious that the generated text is used in the reasoning is it read. Intelligence could be used further, but that is opaque data.

*The author's, 2026-10-07, answering whether reading is counted as a ceiling lowered where a prompt shows it unread.*

Don't include reading time in terms of the change made. The diffs contain the change made, and what of it was read is even harder to imply with confidence, so that might be running further ahead of the honest data we can have. But you could definitely mention these possibilities.

## 8. A pane of bearings, a kiosk, and dimensions

We've done an upgrade with the drag and drop, and that is useful. But one might want to build or ship in a certain mode, more like a kiosk, where you can't move everything around. Especially because the coming feature we definitely need is one pane as one column, not necessarily too wide, holding quite useful information we are very much lacking right now, and it is going to be made of widgets itself. So the default layout would already have a column of multiple widgets, and I think that is a bit too fragile in its current state for everyone. We might come up with an even more functionally flexible framework for that later. Now it doesn't matter; but for the substrate of this idea, it matters.

What do I want in such a pane? All kinds of more information than there is now. Instead of a breadcrumb, I expect the path we are in, line by line: that's a widget. I expect some kind of visual chart of the depth from where I'm standing: this file, the coming files below, and so on. I wish to understand what has a strong connection to this in terms of common links, so that one might understand the various lattices. Say one lattice is workflow and another is goals, and the goals and workflows link a lot to each other. Sure, if the file I am in links to something else, I want to see that there too. But if my parents and my siblings have lots of links, closely related to something else, then I want to understand that as well.

That is what the OpenLight project enables in terms of dimensions, and dimensions are spoken about a bit in some projects based on the rule as well. Say this is the tool's ontology: some of my workflows are added to a dimension of customer experience, and some others to a dimension of admin experience. The workflows might all be in a hierarchy that does not divide them like that, and another slice of division might be that one.

I'm not giving you the full requirements for the possibilities of widgets here; I'm giving you the higher-order goals and requirements. There must be understanding of all manner, because there are so many ways to go about it.

Something that will definitely be helpful here is the addition of dimensions, which I've been thinking about a while, and which has been spoken about in my ideas as well. Currently colour comes only from the root, divided by the root's mounts. Sometimes you specifically want a certain holarchy, the address of a file and its subfiles, to become a space. That is, it is truly a parent: everything under it is connected to that parent, not only through each step of the holarchy but immediately, in terms of instanceship. That truly changes what widgets you can make structurally, and also what kind of visuals you can show, as well as further styling of the dimension you are in and of how things look when you are looking at them. Currently things have just one colour, and that is of course very much too singular.

Write the substrate honouring what I've said here, then go ahead and play a little with what you spawn in there. As we conclude and as we like something, we commit to it; otherwise we continue to iterate. Given that we are in git, you are free to play: work in a structured manner, with commits, so that we may drop things that don't make sense. Work for a while, as long as is realistic for the context window. Under the rule, iterate enough to make sure what you do is of quality and under the rule, and that various subagents, clean heads, and you alike think it is right. If you are not finding confidence because you are finding too much critique in the iteration, you need to improve, and to find stability in that you need to think properly about what you are doing. I trust that you know how to set up the substrate from this prompt and how to care for this process under the rule, and I look forward to coming back from a small vacation to see what you've done under the rule, and to take part in it.
