# The canvas

## 1. Comprehension is not only head-on

Comprehension does not always come from head-on analysis, from untangling a thought while resting on the focus of what is known. A person and an inference machine, as inference works today, are quite different in this. The author woke on 2026-09-15 with a wish, not an argument: a canvas on which to view the substrate and move about in it.

*The author's, 2026-09-15.*

## 2. A few modes, from a menu at the foot

The surface is going to have a few modes, chosen perhaps from a centred menu at its foot. The first is the current lane of prose with its wings. The second is a node-based navigation, and there the exploring begins: a few variants are made and held here, and as they are learned from they are removed or refined.

*The author's, 2026-09-15.*

## 3. Scope, and how far things explode

As now, a reader enters [the scope](lane.md#7-scoping-the-lane) of something and looks within it. In the canvas they may also adjust how far things explode, how many levels open, and that wants to be easy to scrub; the rest propagates. A reasonable default is needed, since the shape is hierarchical: starting from the left there is little, and it grows and grows.

*The author's, 2026-09-15.*

## 4. Why work wants nodes

The need for a node-based layout when working with work is what user experience calls user flows: to see as nodes what would otherwise be the headings of one long flat prose, with its branches, and with the flows that are reused.

With [several parents to a child](../../tool-making/README.md#33-a-child-with-several-parents), the canvas is what fits: a piece of work in front of the reader, a step inside it, and on entering the step the holarchy within. From there the reader goes back up to where they came from, or back up to whatever else leads there.

*The author's, 2026-09-15.*

## 5. Two ways of laying the nodes

The first thought was a page of its own, with each node holding all of its prose. Now it seems a brief, a heading, is always its own node, and how the nodes compose depends on what is folded and opened, so that the area gives each node its proper place.

That suggests two modes: one where the prose is read in place in the nodes and the reader moves about among them; and one where the nodes are condensed and the prose stands to one side.

*The author's, 2026-09-15.*

### 5.1 The prose drawer

In the condensed mode a drawer of prose stands to one side, and in the graph a node is only its heading. Pointing at it gives its face, pressing it gives the reading.

On a linear run of pieces, ordered as they are today, the drawer can simply be scrolled, and scrolling to the next piece moves the focus to the next node and pans the viewport, when needed or always. So the reader scrolls on the right and is taken through the flow. Where the flow branches, the reader has to choose; scrolling up to where several things relate to this one, they have to choose again, though by default they go back the way they came.

This may be the best of both worlds: the normal scrolled reading, and the movement through the nodes for free.

*The author's, 2026-09-15.*

### 5.2 Pull past the top

At the top of a scope, pulling up beyond where the lane can go should fill a gauge, and when it is full the reader is taken up, as some applications refresh when pulled past their top. The same could hold in the canvas mode with the prose to one side. A small detail.

*The author's, 2026-09-15.*

## 6. What this leaves open

Which of the two modes is built first, and whether a mode stands beside the lane or the canvas is a figure in a wing. What a step in the lane itself would test of this before a canvas is drawn, which is [the next thing to be talked through](../../tool-making/README.md#34-this-critiques-the-substrate).
