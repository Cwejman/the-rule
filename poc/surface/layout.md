# The page laid by hand

The page is a row of columns, each a stack of programs, and the reader lays it by dragging: a program by its grip, a width or a height by the gap beside it. Programs belong to the body or to git, a dock holds each once, and each carries a head with its own settings.

Nothing of it is built. Once it is, it supersedes [the areas and the strip](framework.md#2-the-areas), and the history as [a mode](history.md#41-it-is-a-mode-not-a-pane).

*The author's, 2026-10-01, in discussion with the session; where the session proposed and the author let it stand, the brief says so.*

## 1. Why it is laid again

Today five areas stand in a fixed row, and an icon at the foot cycles a widget through nowhere, the left and the right. A wing holds two figures and the middle two panes, and nothing is dragged. So an arrangement is had by a run of presses, some cannot be had at all, and the widths are knobs set away from what they size.

What a reader wants is to put a thing where they want it and to pull it to the size they want, the way a window is moved.

*The author's, 2026-10-01.*

## 2. A row of columns, each a stack

The page is a row of columns from left to right, and a column holds one program or several stacked from top to bottom. That is the whole layout. A wing was a column of figures and the middle the columns holding panes, so neither stands as a place of its own any longer.

*The session's, 2026-10-01, from the wings that already stack two figures.*

### 2.1 Some programs stand alone in their column

A program says how it takes height, as figures already do. The prose stands alone in its column at the page's whole height, since a reading cut short is no reading. A figure that grows shares what is left of its column, and a figure of fixed size takes only what it needs.

*That the prose cannot be split by height is the author's, 2026-10-01; the three kinds, and the canvas and the plate standing alone beside the prose, are the session's.*

### 2.2 The plate and the dish are one

The dish was the plate at the middle's size. Once a column is as wide as the reader pulls it, the plate is one program, and its column says how large it is drawn.

*The session's, 2026-10-01.*

### 2.3 Why a row and not a tree of splits

A tree of splits in two is the most general layout, and a row of stacks is a case of it. It was weighed and not taken: as the screen narrows a tree has no order to give way in, nested halves make three equal columns rescale when one gap moves, and its ratios fight the prose's width. A row gives way along one axis, and every gap between columns is of one kind.

The layout is kept as a tree of any number of children, held to two levels, a row of stacks. If a band across the page is wanted later, the history's trunk perhaps, the bound is lifted and nothing laid before it changes.

*The session's reasoning, 2026-10-01, at the author's question.*

## 3. The gaps are the handles

Every gap between two programs can be dragged. A gap between two columns sets their widths, and a gap between two programs in a stack sets their heights. Nothing new is drawn for it: the pointer says it can pull there, and the gap setting stays what it is, the least room between two programs.

So the knobs that set a width go: the prose's measure is the width of its column, and the canvas's greatest width is where its gap was left. A gap pressed twice gives its programs back their own widths, the prose's measure as it stands by default and a figure's declared width.

*The author's, 2026-10-01, that dragging between programs, across and down, replaces the knobs, and that the gap stays a knob; the double press the session's.*

### 3.1 Centred, or the room spread between

Where the row is narrower than the viewport, what is left is a setting. Kept to the centre, the room stands at the two edges and the row in the middle; spread, it is shared among the gaps between programs, as the page does today. Pulling the gap at either edge draws the whole row in from both sides, so a centred row stays centred.

*The author's, 2026-10-01.*

### 3.2 Giving way

As the screen narrows, the programs that grow shrink to their least width first, and then whole columns give way, the one furthest from the program holding the keys first. A column given way is quiet in the dock, as an area given no room is today.

*The session's, 2026-10-01.*

## 4. A program is moved by dragging it

A program is taken by its grip, which stands at the left of its [head](#7-every-program-has-a-head), or anywhere on it with the alt key held. The grip is for whoever does not know the key, the key for whoever does.

While it is dragged nothing on the page moves. The program under the pointer shades one zone of itself: its left or right half to stand beside it as a new column, its top or bottom half to stand in its stack, its middle to trade places. A program that stands alone shades no top or bottom, and is never dropped into a stack. Let go, and the program stands in the zone shaded.

*The author's, 2026-10-01, that moving is by drag and that both the grip and the key are needed; the one shaded zone, and nothing moving until it is let go, the session's.*

## 5. The body and git, two worlds

A program belongs to one of two worlds: the body, or git. The body's prose, canvas and plate keep in step with each other, git's prose and canvas with each other, and each world is [the one shared state](framework.md#4-one-shared-state-and-a-few-verbs) kept once for itself. So the history is no longer a mode the panes are turned to, and its switch and its key go: its prose and its canvas are programs that stand beside the body's.

The worlds meet in one place: a commit lights the briefs it touched in the body's programs, as the plate already lights them. The keys act in the world of the program that holds them, and the page's address stays the body's.

Today the history's world is set in place of the body's while it is read. Both standing at once means the page holds two worlds together, which is the real work this asks of the code.

*The author's, 2026-10-01, that git's prose and canvas are programs of their own and that what belongs to one world stays together; the meeting place and the cost are the session's.*

### 5.1 A program of git's is told apart by its grip

Git's prose already reads as stories and commits, and its canvas as commits in their hues. So no frame is drawn around them, and the page stays flat: a program of git's keeps its grip standing when the pointer is away, so git's mark always stands at its top edge.

*The author asked whether git's programs need chrome of their own to be told apart, 2026-10-01; the grip in its place is the session's, and the author let it stand.*

## 6. The dock

The dock holds every program once, in groups set apart by room: git's status, git's prose and git's canvas; then the prose, the canvas and the plate; then the figures, the shape, the ahead, the tree and the settings. It stands down the left edge or along the foot, which is a setting.

A press on a program's icon takes it away, and a second press puts it back where it stood, beside the nearest program that still stands there if its own place is gone. An icon dragged from the dock onto the page is placed by the same shaded zones as a program moved, and a program dragged onto the dock is taken away.

*The author's, 2026-10-01; where a program returns once its neighbours have moved is the session's.*

### 6.1 Git's status is a program

Git's status says whether the diff is shown, with something more of it, and a press on it may tell more of the diff. What it shows is not defined yet.

*The author's, 2026-10-01, left open by the author.*

## 7. Every program has a head

A program's head is a thin row along its top edge: the grip at its left, and after it the settings of that program alone, quiet in [the strip's four weights](framework.md#31-four-weights-of-ink). It stands in the fade the prose already has at its top, so it covers nothing read.

It shows when the pointer comes near the program's top edge rather than whenever the pointer is on the program, since a reader with a mouse resting on the prose would otherwise read under it the whole time. Leaving, it goes quiet before it goes, so a pointer on its way to it still finds it.

What each head holds:

| Program | Its head |
|---|---|
| prose, the body's or git's | the gutter at its left and at its right, each holding nothing, the links or another adjunct |
| canvas, the body's or git's | the face, and which way its root's level runs |
| plate | nothing yet |
| git's status | not defined yet |

So the gutters leave the dock and become a setting of each prose, and both proses can carry links. The canvas's two acts at its foot today, the face and the root across, move into its head. The settings program keeps only what belongs to the whole page: the zoom, the gap, the fade, the dim, the centring and the dock's edge.

*The author's, 2026-10-01, that a program's own settings stand where its grip stands and show on pointing, and that a gutter is a setting of each prose; the head showing only near the top edge the session's, which the author let stand.*

## 8. What this leaves open

Layouts kept by number and called by a key were proposed and not answered. Whether a gap is as easy to hit as the gap setting makes it, or needs a least width of its own to be found, is not known until it is tried. Where the layout is kept is assumed to be the browser's storage, as the settings are.

A phone keeps [its own reading](touch.md) and none of this; whether a head or a drag has any place there is not asked yet. And what git's status shows is left for later.

*The session's, 2026-10-01.*
