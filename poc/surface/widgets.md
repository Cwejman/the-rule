# The widgets

The widgets are what can stand in a wing or a gutter: the tree, the shape, the ahead, the links, the settings, the plate and the keys, each described once it exists. They keep one set of rules between them, so a reader learns a family rather than a stranger per figure.

*Reasoned, 2026-09-13; each widget carries its own grade beneath.*

## 1. What every widget keeps

Every shape is computed from the substrate and none is invented, because a picture a reader cannot trust is worse than no picture at all. What the widgets share beyond that is the rules beneath, and keeping them is what makes seven drawings one family.

*Reasoned, 2026-09-13, carried whole from the first model's figures.*

### 1.1 Nothing is drawn too small to read

Every figure has a floor, the smallest cell a pointer can hit and an eye can place, and rather than shrink through it a figure shows less: it merges what it cannot draw into one cell and marks that the cell holds more.

That is the gradient turned on a picture, and it is [the design language's rhythm floor](https://github.com/Cwejman/OpenLight/blob/main/@md/spec/design.md#rhythm--depth-derived-never-stated-twice) met in a figure instead of in a layout. A figure that is for the whole body may go beneath the floor, and says so where it is described.

*Reasoned, 2026-09-13.*

### 1.2 No figure mixes its two sizes

A brief has two sizes: its own prose, which is what stopping to read it costs, and that prose with everything beneath it, which is what the branch holds. A row shows at most the first, and a figure of branches, one that draws the levels beneath a brief, shows the second.

*Reasoned, 2026-09-13.*

### 1.3 An image is never passed off as prose

Wherever a figure draws a brief's blocks it draws an image as a frame, where a paragraph is a filled block, and the caption beneath the image as the text it is. A figure that draws blocks as [the lane](lane.md) presents them draws the image at the size it is presented, since a figure that scrubs the lane has to stay true to it, and a figure that weighs a brief weighs its images [as the weight setting says](#62-the-switches).

*Reasoned, the author's, 2026-09-14.*

### 1.4 One brief is lit wherever it is drawn

Every widget draws the same briefs from the same sizes, so the brief highlighted, [the focus or the one under the pointer](lane.md#4-where-you-are-is-the-brief-in-focus), lights in all of them at once. Where a figure has merged past the brief being pointed at, the cell holding it is lit instead.

A light eases in and out rather than snapping, so the eye follows where it went: a cell of [the plate](#7-the-plate) and its glow, a card's rim and ink, and a box on the canvas over about a sixth of a second. In prose, a lit title, number or link takes a tenth, since a reader moving the pointer across words should not see a trail of fading ink behind it. Every move eases, a scroll as much as a press or the pointer: the plate drawn again as the focus moves keeps the cells it has already laid and changes only their marks, and a widget drawn wholly anew shows its marks at once. A glow comes and goes at once, since one eased from nothing passes through black before it takes its hue.

*Reasoned, 2026-09-13. The easing the author's ask, 2026-10-12, quick on prose; its times the session's, until the author has looked.*

### 1.5 Ink is spent only where a reader has asked

No widget repeats what is in view, and a mark is spent in proportion to how live the fact behind it is, which is [the design language's measure](https://github.com/Cwejman/OpenLight/blob/main/@md/spec/design.md#the-graduated-scale--one-mark-per-live-fact). Colour says only where in the body a thing sits, by [its slice of the colour wheel](#18-a-brief-is-its-slice-of-the-colour-wheel), or as a trial where it sits within the scope.

*Reasoned, 2026-09-13.*

### 1.6 One tooltip, beneath the pointer

A cell in any figure, a step or a cell of the way down, an icon on a strip, and a link in the lane when no gutter tells of it, each says what it is a moment after the pointer rests on it: for a brief, its path drawn as the way down draws one, then its name, and for a link the opening words of its face.

It is one element on the page's own ground, never under the pointer, and it goes with any scroll or press. Where it would hide what is looked at it moves aside: beside a figure, on the side nearest the lane and level with the cell, since a tooltip over the shape covers the very cell pointed at; beside a node on the canvas, clear of the reading it opens; and beneath anything else. The ahead keeps naming its pointed cell beneath the figure as well.

*Reasoned, the author's ask of 2026-09-15; [an overlay that repeats what is in view](refused.md#j-an-overlay-at-all) stays refused, and this tells only what is not in view.*

### 1.7 A key stands as a badge where its act stands

An act the reader can take stands on the page as a badge: its key drawn as a cap, and what the act does beside it in a word. It stands where the act's object stands, [on the brief's own line](lane.md#61-the-action-line-is-the-press) or on the strip it moves, so a reader meets the few acts that are at hand rather than a bar of every act there is.

The keys are drawn rather than set in type, since a shift, a return and an arrow taken from three faces sit at three heights and no two weigh the same. Each is one path in one box, so a row of caps reads level. Where the room is tight the badge is its keys alone, since a badge that stands on the very thing it acts on has its place to say what it does, and two acts that share a modifier stand as one unit with the modifier drawn once, so a pair reads as the pair it is.

Beside a brief the cap says how that brief is reached. On the brief the reading line stands on it shows the key, since that is the brief a key acts on; on every other it shows the pointer, which is the only way to that one. The two take the same room, so no line shifts as the reading moves.

A badge that cannot be taken now keeps its room and goes quiet, so a row never shifts under the pointer. Pressing one does what its key does, and [the tooltip](#16-one-tooltip-beneath-the-pointer) tells the rest: what the act does, what the same key with shift does, and the other ways to the same thing.

Where there is no key to draw, the badge carries the word alone, which is [its third grain](touch.md#8-a-badge-without-a-key); and where it stands in a row of round buttons, its glyph alone, which is [the fourth](touch.md#462-the-foot-is-one-row-and-the-card-is-what-stands-behind-it).

*The author's ask of 2026-09-16, that every act a key fires be visible where it makes sense; the glyphs drawn rather than set, the author's ask the same day.*

### 1.8 A brief is its slice of the colour wheel

Colour says where in the body a thing sits, at every depth, so it is laid by the branching: every brief is a slice of the colour wheel, and its colour is that slice.

The root is the whole wheel. A brief shares its slice equally among the briefs directly beneath it: the root's seven parts take a seventh each, and a part with six sections gives each a sixth of its own. The share follows the branching and never the size, since how much a branch holds says nothing of how much it matters. Siblings' slices meet edge to edge and fill their parent's, so no two siblings share one.

So a brief's colour narrows as the reader goes down. A part of the root spans a wide run of the wheel, a section of it a few degrees, and a brief a few levels deeper a sliver that reads as one hue. Below where a difference can be seen there is none, and that is the gradient met in colour: the most difference at the top, where it says the most.

A fill wide enough that a run of colour can be seen across it, as most cells of [the plate](#7-the-plate) near its centre are, takes its slice whole, as a run across it. So does a rim drawn around a card in its slice's hue: [the lane's card](lane.md#12-a-placed-part-raises-a-card) where the reading stands on it or it is lit by a pointer on it anywhere, and [the canvas's box](canvas.md#45-a-row-keeps-its-edge-and-says-what-it-leads-to-by-it) that leads to a reading, heads one, is selected, is where the reading stands or is under the pointer. A box with the quiet rim keeps it. The run is read along the rim, the slice going round the wheel from the card's left edge to its right, and never across the rim's weight, since a card is wide where a word is short. The root's rim, the whole wheel, runs all of it, as the scope's does under [the trial](#181-a-trial-colour-from-the-scope). In text, where a ring stands close around a name, it takes the middle, as the name's ink does.

Everything else, a name's ink, a line, an underline, a mark or a small cell, takes the middle of its slice, since a run across a word or a hairline reads as noise rather than as place. Ink can therefore match where slices nest: a parent with an odd number of children shares its middle with the middle one, and slices too thin to tell apart read alike. The root, the whole wheel, has no middle, and its ink keeps the hue of nought degrees it has always had.

The wheel begins with the first part's middle at thirty degrees, the warm red it has always taken, and the parts follow round in their order, so neighbours stand near in hue; with seven parts, each middle stands about 51 degrees from the next.

Whether a slice is shared among every brief or among files alone is [a switch](#62-the-switches) of its own, and the plate's choice of a cell per brief or per file, in its head or forced where cells are too small, never changes it. Among files, a file's slice is shared among the files placed in it, and every section takes the slice of its file. A body whose root places no files is shared among every brief whatever the switch, since among files it would be one hue throughout.

[Git's history](history.md) is coloured the same way on a wheel of its own, its stories and commits dividing the history's root down its branching as the body's briefs divide theirs, while a reading a commit touched is named in the colour it has in the body.

Until then each part of the root took one hue, stepped around the wheel by the golden angle so that neighbours stood far apart. That ink takes the middle and a wide fill the run, that the wheel begins at the first part's old hue, and that the switch stands apart from the plate's, are the session's, until the author has looked. The author's, the same day, asked whether text could carry the run and, if not, that the cards at least should: the rims took it. As built the same day: a cell of the plate shows its run where its slice spans three degrees and the cell twelve pixels along, in wedges of two degrees.*

#### 1.8.1 A trial: colour from the scope

As a trial a reader turns on, the wheel can be laid from the scope instead of from the root. Then the scope's brief is the whole wheel and takes its ink as the root does, and the briefs beneath it share it as the root's would. At the root it changes nothing.

Everything outside the scope is drawn in grey, its lightness kept and its hue taken away: the levels above it, wherever the way down, a map drawn from the root or a plate drawn whole shows them, and every branch off the way to it.

So colour says where a thing sits within the scope, at the most difference the wheel can give there, and says only that everything else is elsewhere. It suits [a map drawn from the scope](canvas.md#64-a-map-may-be-drawn-from-the-scope-as-the-plate-may) and the plate drawn the same way, where little or nothing outside the scope stands, and neither sets the other.

Among files, a scope that places no files is shared among every brief beneath it, as a root that places none is. Git's history is laid from git's own scope where a reader has scoped it, and from its root where not, and a reading a commit touched takes the colour it has in the body, grey where it lies outside the body's scope.

It is [a switch](#62-the-switches), off unless a reader turns it on, and a trial: what a body should be coloured by is the question [dimensions](../../author/ideas-2026-10-07.md#8-a-pane-of-bearings-a-kiosk-and-dimensions) will answer, and this is a way to look at one answer before they do.

*The author's ask, 2026-10-12, as an opt-in trial to explore while dimensions with their visuals are further off. That everything outside the scope is grey and not only the levels above it, that the lightness is kept with the hue alone taken away, and that the scope's brief takes the root's ink, are the session's. Open: not yet built.*

#### 1.8.2 The ground and the headings may take the hue

In prose the only steady tell of a brief's colour is its heading's number, since the heading itself stands in ink and a card's rim shows only where the reading stands on it. So two [meters](#61-the-meters), each at nought until a reader turns it, let the colour be felt in the reading itself, as strongly as the reader wants, and they work alone or together.

The ground takes the reading open's slice of the wheel laid down the reading's length, and shows the part of it the screen stands over: the top of the screen takes the hue as far into the slice as the screen's top is into the reading, and its foot likewise, so the ground says where the reader is within the reading as a scrollbar would, in the colour the briefs there take. At the root the slice is the whole wheel, so the ground passes through each part's hue as the reader passes its card; a reading a few levels down runs nearly one hue. The meter sets how strongly, from plain to the most it gives. The page is the ground everywhere it shows, so it is felt under the prose, the gutters, the lane's cards and the way down alike; where the wheel is laid from the scope, a reading outside it is grey.

On that ground the greys take the hue too, as far as the ground does: the ink, the muted and the faint keep their lightness and lean to the hue, since a neutral grey on a coloured ground reads as something laid over it rather than written on it. Prose dimmed or faded lets the ground through, and blends with it.

A card on that ground is a thing of its own colour standing on it, never a pane the ground shows through: it takes its slice whole as a run, as its rim does, a step from the ground, so at the root each part's card is its own hue over the wheel rather than glass over it.

A heading's title is drawn as far from ink towards its branch's ink as its meter says, the opening's title with it, and a lit one leans as far again towards the lit tone.

Other means were weighed and are not built: a hairline in the hue down the lane's edge beside each brief, which a reader's eye does not cross while reading; the reading line drawn in the focus's hue; and a faint wash behind the brief in focus alone, which says what the way down and the plate already say.

## 2. The tree

The tree is a [wing](framework.md#2-the-areas) widget: [the way down](lane.md#75-the-way-down-stands-over-the-lane) laid line by line, and beneath its last step the reading open as an outline. What the way down says on one line it says here with the room to say more, in the language [a canvas drawn as text](canvas.md#411-a-node-may-be-one-line-of-text-joined-as-a-file-tree-is) already draws in.

*The author's, 2026-10-14: the tree was hardly used, being older than the way down and the canvas's text, and is the way down made larger once it is drawn as they are. Until then it was the lane alone as an outline, with the levels above the scope as dim rows over its root.*

### 2.1 The way, line by line

The way from the root to the reading open stands at the top, each step nested beneath the one before it, and each told as the way down tells it.

The root stands as the ring of the whole wheel, a reading bold, a section at normal weight after its number, what stands above the reading open muted. The reading open stands in its branch's ink, with its status and the bar of how far through it the focus stands.

The lines join the steps as a file tree's do, a line down the left and a tick into each row. Where a step crosses into a file the line into it is drawn in the hue of the reading entered, a step stronger, as a canvas draws an opening; within a file it is the quiet line.

Pressing a step goes there, and pressing one above the reading open takes the lane out to it.

*The author's ask, 2026-10-14; how each is drawn is the session's, read from the way down and the canvas's text.*

### 2.2 What stands beside the way

Each step of the way but the root says after its name how many stand beside it in its level, and pressing that lays the level whole in place.

The step then stands among its siblings, so a reader sees what the way passed by without scrolling the lane or leaving the reading. Pressed again, the level closes back to the step. A sibling is a row as any row of the outline, its number, its name and the figure of what it hides, and pressing it goes there.

What is laid open beside the way is the tree's own and lasts only while the page is open: it moves nothing in the lane.

*The session's, 2026-10-14, proposed to the author, who let it stand.*

### 2.3 The reading open, as an outline

Beneath the way the reading open stands as the lane holds it: one row per brief in the lane, nested under its parent, unfolded where the brief is whole. It is not a second state: folding a row folds the brief in the lane and unfolding one unfolds it, so what the tree shows is exactly what the lane holds, and a reader folds a whole region from the tree without scrolling to it.

A row carries its number faint before its name, and after it, where it is folded or is a card, [the figure of what it hides](canvas.md#46-a-folded-node-says-how-deep-it-is) at about half its length, as text does on the canvas. A card is underlined in its branch's hue, as a name that opens onto a reading of its own is, with its status after it. The rows on the way to the focus take their branch's ink.

Pressing a row's name goes to the brief. A line lies across the highlighted row and glides as the highlight moves. The wing scrolls only to keep the focus in view, driven by the lane and never free.

*Reasoned, the author's, 2026-09-13; [columns of siblings](refused.md#o-the-path-as-columns-of-siblings-stacked) and [a tree with an unfolding of its own](refused.md#u-a-tree-with-an-unfolding-of-its-own) were built first and refused. The numbers, the figures and the cards as the canvas's text draws them, the author's ask of 2026-10-14. It does not stand on a phone, where it [read poorly whole](touch.md#42-the-outline-and-the-radial-are-not-ready-for-a-finger), 2026-09-16.*

What lies beside the way across the body rather than in it: the links the reading open and its neighbours share with other parts, which [the pane of bearings](../../author/ideas-2026-10-07.md#8-a-pane-of-bearings-a-kiosk-and-dimensions) asks for and dimensions would ground. ## 3. The shape

The shape is the left wing's first widget: the lane as laid, drawn small, so the reader sees the shape of the prose and the nesting it stands in at once, and fold state shows as length.

Each brief in the lane stands as its blocks, a heading a short bar and a paragraph a block as tall as it is drawn, shifted right by the brief's depth. Every block a brief has is drawn, a list or a table as readily as a paragraph, and an image as a frame as wide and as tall as it stands in the lane.

Where every other area has given way it stands narrow, as [the rail](touch.md#5-the-minimap-is-a-rail-not-a-mode), nesting a step tighter and giving its marks whatever room each row has left.

It is as wide as it draws: its rows as the lane lays them and the marks of each folded brief, so it takes no room it leaves bare, and it widens and narrows as the lane is scoped and folded. The ghost of what an unfolded brief would hide, drawn only under the pointer, reaches out over the gap beside it rather than keeping room of its own.

*Reasoned, the author's, 2026-09-13, from the wish for a minimap that moves the briefs and their paragraphs right and left by where they stand in the nesting; the rail, 2026-09-16. The author's, 2026-10-05, that it takes only the width its drawing uses; the ghosts reaching over the gap are the session's.*

### 3.1 A folded brief tells what it hides

Beside its face a folded brief carries a grey tick for every paragraph and a small grey frame for every image, as many as the room beside it holds, then, where a level lies beneath, a grey tail as long as that level is heavy. So a reader sees at a glance where the lane is folded, whether paragraphs or levels wait there, and how much.

*Reasoned, the author's, 2026-09-13.*

### 3.2 A card says the lane stops there

[A card](lane.md#12-a-placed-part-raises-a-card) is not a brief of this reading, and the shape draws it apart: a row set off from the rows around it, holding the marks of the brief on the other side, with nothing beneath it however heavy that branch is. A tail would say the level waits one fold away, and it does not; it waits one opening away.

So a reader scanning the shape sees where this reading ends before they reach it, and the two things a press can do, folding within and crossing out, never look alike.

*The author's ask of 2026-09-18, that the minimap embody the boundary; the form as built.*

### 3.3 The levels above the scope

When the lane is scoped, the levels above the scope stand to the left of the opening's row, a grey tick per ancestor, outermost leftmost, and each is a press that scopes out to that level, as the ticks to the right are what a fold hides.

*Reasoned, the author's, 2026-09-13.*

### 3.4 The viewport scrubs the lane

The viewport, the part of the lane on the screen, is drawn over the shape as a shaded band, and dragging it scrubs the lane, so the shape is also the way to move fast through a long body.

*Reasoned, the author's, 2026-09-13.*

### 3.5 Two presses, read from where they land

A brief has two presses, and what a press does is read from where it lands, so no modifier key is needed. Its blocks go to it. The room to their right, where its ticks and tail stand when it is folded and nothing stands when it is unfolded, folds or unfolds it, and folding from there never scrolls the lane.

The room reaches only as far as the marks do, and keeps to its own row, since rows touch and a room reaching into its neighbour would light one row while pointing at another. A row thinner than six pixels is given six there, so it can be hit.

Pointing at the room points at the brief, so [the ahead](#4-the-ahead) shows what the brief hides before the press unfolds it, and the brief lights elsewhere as it would. In the shape itself its blocks stay unlit and only the marks answer: over a folded brief its ticks and tail light, and over an unfolded one they show as a grey ghost of what a fold would hide. So resting on the room reads apart from resting on the blocks.

Neither press is there under a finger, where [the rail takes none](touch.md#53-the-rail-takes-no-presses) and is scrubbed instead.

*Reasoned, the author's, 2026-09-13; the room pointing at its brief, 2026-09-14, so the ahead previews a fold from there too; the rail without presses, 2026-09-16.*

## 4. The ahead

The ahead is the right wing's first widget: what the highlighted brief hides. It answers for the focus, or for the brief under the pointer, whenever that brief is folded and hides something, paragraphs beyond its face or a level beneath, and it draws nothing at all when the brief is whole, since then the reader can simply read on. It stops where the lane stops: what a card leads to waits past a boundary and no fold reaches it, so the ahead never draws it.

*Reasoned, the author's, 2026-09-13; built first [as an icicle](refused.md#s-the-ahead-as-columns-by-depth), which the author narrowed to what is not in view.*

### 4.1 It draws in the shape's form

It draws [as the shape draws](#3-the-shape), so a reader learns one figure and not two: first the brief itself with the paragraphs its face hides, then everything beneath it, each brief a heading bar and its paragraphs as blocks, shifted right by nesting. So unfolding a brief that is only more paragraphs is understood as exactly that, before it is pressed.

No names are drawn in the cells; the cell under the pointer is named beneath the figure, and above it the brief whose hidden part is drawn is named by its whole path, since the path is its address. The figure is only as large as what it draws needs, and it keeps its root while the pointer moves within it, so it does not redraw under the pointer. Pressing a cell goes there, which unfolds it.

*Reasoned, the author's ask of 2026-09-13.*

### 4.2 A ladder down when it does not fit

Where what it draws is too large for its paragraphs to stay legible it steps down a ladder that never loses the structure.

First, one block per brief, its own size, with every brief still in place. Where that still does not fit, the deepest level is dropped and the brief that held it carries a grey tail as long as the dropped level is heavy, [as the shape marks a fold](#31-a-folded-brief-tells-what-it-hides), until what is left fits. The caption says which step it stands on.

*Reasoned, the author's, 2026-09-13; [a step that merged a whole level into one block](refused.md#v-a-level-merged-into-one-block) was refused.*

## 5. Links

Links is the right [gutter's](framework.md#2-the-areas) first widget. Beside each link in a brief, on the link's own line, it tells the target: where it stands, its whole path drawn as the way down draws one, then its title and the opening words of its face, or that it leads out of the body, or that the brief is not yet written.

The link itself is underlined in its target's branch hue, so the colour says where a link leads before it is pointed at. At the brief's foot it tells what points at the brief. Pointing at any of them lights the target wherever it is drawn, and pressing goes.

This is what lets looking be free without [an overlay](refused.md#j-an-overlay-at-all). Everything an overlay told about a link now stands beside the link, and everything it told about a brief stands in the lane or in the wings.

As the width tightens [the gutter narrows](framework.md#28-giving-way) rather than moving anywhere, so what stands beside a link stays beside it, and only the opening words are cut shorter to keep the column a column.

*Reasoned, the author's, 2026-09-13; the narrowing the author's ask of 2026-09-16, which retired a figure that carried these into the wing as a list for the brief in focus.*

## 6. Settings

Settings is a wing widget that edits [the settings](framework.md#5-settings-are-data-and-a-figure-edits-them) by eye against real prose, since the page redraws as a value changes. It is meters for what has a range and switches for what has a few values.

*Reasoned, the author's, 2026-09-13; [a dial](refused.md#w-a-dial) was built first and refused.*

### 6.1 The meters

Each meter is a 270 degree arc with rounded ends, the value drawn in a warm red over a faint track, its icon in the middle, and its name and value told on pointing. Dragging a meter up or down turns it and the wheel steps it. A drag turns a meter in place and never redraws the wing under the pointer.

The first row sets the type: the zoom, the heading ratio, the headings' hue and the line height. The second sets the page: the gap, the dim, the ground's hue and the edge fade. The two hues stand at nought until a reader turns them, and [what they colour](#182-the-ground-and-the-headings-may-take-the-hue) is said with the colour. The space beneath a heading grows with the heading, so a larger ratio opens the room between a heading and its prose as well.

*Reasoned, the author's, 2026-09-13. The measure's meter and the canvas's width went on 2026-10-01, since [the gaps](layout.md#3-the-gaps-are-the-handles) size them. The two hues, the author's ask of 2026-10-14.*

### 6.2 The switches

Each switch is a setting's name and its few values, a row apiece. The theme is light, dark, or whatever the system is set to, which is where a reader starts; how the two themes are composed is [one palette with two sides](implementation.md#64-one-palette-two-sides). The reading line stands [at the middle or easing to the ends](lane.md#41-where-the-reading-line-stands). [The flick](lane.md#66-the-flick-a-trial) is on or off.

The ahead answers for a brief that hides something, or always, for any highlighted brief with anything beneath it, so a reader may preview every level as they move. The weight is cost or experience: what a brief weighs in the tails and the plate. Cost is its text alone, which is what reading it costs and where a reader starts. Experience counts its images as well, as the text that would fill their room at the default measure. The colour is every brief or files: whether [a brief's slice of the colour wheel](#18-a-brief-is-its-slice-of-the-colour-wheel) is shared among the briefs directly beneath it or among the files placed in it, and a reader starts with every brief. ## 7. The plate

The plate is a wing widget: the body whole in one round figure, the root at its centre and every other brief a cell standing on its parent's outer edge, so the way from the root to any brief is a run of cells edge to edge, each level a ring around the one inside it. The path to the focus and the focus are marked on it as everywhere, and what is folded out of the lane is pale at every depth.

Pressing a cell goes there, and pressing with the modifier key held, command or control, folds or unfolds it in place. It does not stand on a phone, where [no finger can land on a cell that small](touch.md#42-the-outline-and-the-radial-are-not-ready-for-a-finger).

It draws the body whole, or, set in its head, only the reading the reader is scoped to, that reading's own brief at the centre. Where it has the room to show a cell per brief, its head also sets whether it does or shows a cell per file only; smaller than that it is files alone, and the head offers no choice.

At the middle's size it is [the dish](framework.md#26-the-middle-holds-one-pane-or-two), a pane of its own, which is where a body too large for a wing's plate has the room to be seen a cell per brief.

*Reasoned, the author's, 2026-09-12, carried from [the first program's figures](README.md#6-the-programs-before-this-one), and laid again as a plate of cells on 2026-09-22, with the dish the same day; off the phone since 2026-09-16. Wide, from 2026-10-01, the plate and the dish are [one program](layout.md#22-the-plate-and-the-dish-are-one-program), drawn as the dish once its column is pulled wide enough. The author's, 2026-10-05, that it may follow the scope or stand global, and that a cell per file or per brief is a choice where the room allows one.*

### 7.1 A cell's room is how many stand beneath it

A cell's share of its parent's edge is how many cells stand beneath it, and each ring's area follows how many cells it holds, so a crowded level has the room it needs and a level of few is not drawn as a band of wide slabs. The room between two cells is their kinship's: a hairline within a file, more between two files, most between two branches.

It is laid once for each body, still and with nothing random in it, so the same body always lays the same plate.

*As built, 2026-09-22. It replaced, the same day, a trial of droplets pushed apart and drawn back to their rings, and with it the plate of 2026-09-12, droplets cut by their neighbours; why the trial gave way was said in talk and is not written, and the session that writes this did not hear it.*

### 7.2 A plate too small for a cell per brief stands a cell per file

Where the median cell would stand less than three pixels across, the plate stands a cell per file instead: the root and each placed file's own brief, each file sharing its parent's edge by how many files stand beneath it. A wing's plate of a large body is mostly this.

So nothing is drawn too thin to see, which is [the floor every figure keeps](#11-nothing-is-drawn-too-small-to-read), met by showing less rather than by shrinking through it.

*As built, 2026-09-22; it supersedes the plate of 2026-09-13 that drew every level to the rim as slivers.*

### 7.3 A cell marks the focus it holds

A cell may be a whole file, so the plate marks its own focus: the deepest cell drawn on the way to the focus is the one that holds it, wherever in the file the reader stands, and the cells on the way to it are marked too. Pointing at a cell lights the way to it from the root, cell by cell, where the other figures light the one alone.

*As built, 2026-09-22.*

### 7.4 Every branch reads by its hue in every state

The plate is mostly colour, so its tones stand close together, each just under what the weakest hue on the wheel holds at its lightness: no branch the colour gives a hue goes dull where another stays vivid, whether a cell is marked, pale or lit.

A cell marked or lit lights up as a coloured lamp does, and never darkens: on a light ground each step toward where the reader stands is lighter than the cell at rest, its colour carried by a halo of its hue around it, the widest around the cell they stand on; on a dark ground each step is brighter, as it always was. Lighter, a cell holds less colour, since the weakest hue holds less there, which is why the halo carries it.

*As built, 2026-09-22, the palette's rule of [even chroma](implementation.md#642-colour-stays-even-across-branches-by-chroma) met in the plate's own register. The lamp the author's, 2026-10-12, on seeing a lit cell darken on a light ground; its tones the session's, until the author has looked.*
