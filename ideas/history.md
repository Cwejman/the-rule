# Reading the substrate as a diff

## 1. What was built, and taken out the same day

On 2026-09-17 the surface was given history: every brief its span of lines, git blame saying which of the last sixty commits last touched them, each block of prose the age of its own lines, and a hue per commit on one section of the wheel, the newest warmest, with everything older grey at its own lightness wherever a brief is drawn. The commits then stood in the body as a level of briefs, one per commit, each leading by links to every brief it touched with the first line of the change, with an act to read the body back to that commit and a widget as the level's miniature.

It was taken out that evening, whole, in one commit, before anything was pushed to [the dependents](../repository.md#6-the-steps-in-order). The commits `13e893b`, `0d31287`, `e474196` and `7822ec8` hold the work, and the revert that took it out names them, so it can be brought back when its questions are answered.

*Seen, 2026-09-17; the author's decision to take it out, after looking.*

## 2. Why it came out

The waiting time. Blaming every stamped file on every retrace put two and a half seconds on each request for the body, and the first request after a start read sixty commits' diffs and took eleven. A page that inlines the body as data cannot carry that at all: the built page would either be heavy with history or lie about not having it. The live process could be made fast, computing on demand and keeping each answer, but that only mends the live half.

The mixing. Git's account of how the files came to be is a second substrate, and laying it into the body as briefs made the reading of the files carry the reading of their history: the shape grew a level nobody wrote, the check counted briefs that stand in no file, and a reader who wanted the prose met the log. The author's direction after looking is that history is a view apart, opened from its widget, never part of the usual substrate.

The comprehension. Sixty commits in a list, each a long subject, did not help anyone understand what changed. What is wanted is not yet designed: commits by the holon they touched, changes grouped under one colour, a practical overview of what a commit did before a complete one, and the question of when a reader wants this at all. The many commits a day the practice makes are themselves the reason a visual aid is wanted, and the author counts that as a failure to learn from rather than a feature to build over.

*The author's, 2026-09-17, in talk; the measurements the session's.*

## 3. What holds

The data layer's shape held and is worth keeping in mind: blame gives every line its commit, so a brief's age and a block's age fall out of its span without any diff arithmetic, and what a commit touched is read from the commit's own diff mapped to the headings as they stood then, which is exact where blame ranks are not. One palette carried the whole colouring with two variables, the hue and a chroma multiplier, and nothing had to be drawn twice.

The direction stands in the author's ideas: [history built in](../author/ideas-2026-09-17.md#2-history-is-built-in), [commits are briefs](../author/ideas-2026-09-17.md#3-commits-are-briefs), and [commits visible](../author/ideas-2026-09-16.md#25-commits-are-visible). What is postponed is the building, until the data layer is resolved for a built page and the view is designed for comprehension.

*Reasoned, 2026-09-17, the session's; postponed by the author's decision.*
