# The primitives

In making tools a few simple ontological primitives are uncovered, and only what is needed at this stage is extracted. No complete body of existing practice is taken on: not the full approach of user experience with its flows, scenarios and personas. The primitives are laid win by win under the rule.

Goals come first, and the higher goals are unconditional. They may change, but they are much more stable. A goal breaks down into situation and condition, and there it stops being "we want to do this" and becomes "we do this here, because of that".

Work is one word. Studying an existing product finds a great deal of it, under the names user flow, user scenario and user story, and all of it is work. Work is recursive: a piece of work may be reused across other work.

Roles already exist in an existing product. In building one they are not set early on; they come up as the work progresses, step by step.

## 1. Goals give the tool its purpose

A goal says what should become or remain true. The highest goals are unconditional: they express the enduring good without carrying today's process, roles or tool inside them.

Goals become more situated as understanding deepens. Conditions such as scope, timing and proof stand beneath or beside them rather than being mistaken for the goal itself.

## 2. Work is one recursive kind

Work is what is done in service of a goal. A whole undertaking can break into smaller work, and each part can break again as far as understanding needs.

Scenario, process, workflow, task and action do not yet need separate kinds. A distinction is added only when treating them as work no longer preserves something important.

### 2.1 Shared ground rises to a shared level

A piece of work may stand beneath one larger work when its meaning exists within that whole. When several works stand on the same work, it keeps one home at the nearest level that covers them all.

It rises only as far as necessary. At that shared level, the gradient places it before what depends on it, and each consumer links to its one home. Role, channel, subject and time remain valid views but do not move or copy the work.

### 2.2 A grouping must earn its level

Nesting work says that the parts together fulfil a larger work whose goal makes them easier to comprehend. A grouping by role, channel, subject or time does not earn a level merely because it offers a useful view.

Where no larger work gives the parts a more comprehensible whole, they remain flat at their nearest shared level and link through the other views. The hierarchy is then a reading laid by the gradient, not a claim that every visible grouping is absolute.

## 3. Roles locate responsibility today

A role is where responsibility, judgment and the comprehension needed for that judgment presently meet. It records the current arrangement without prescribing how a future solution divides the work.

Responsibility is the relation that says a role answers for a goal. Work may involve several roles, and a person may occupy several roles, so roles link to work rather than containing it.

## 4. The tool emerges through requirements and design

A goal becomes a requirement when it is made binding on a design or implementation. The highest goals can stand before a solution, but situated requirements cannot be completed in isolation from design.

Each design step introduces the least tool needed to test the present understanding. What it reveals changes the goals, work and requirements before the next step. More solution than the question needs biases what follows and lets early choices masquerade as requirements.

## 5. Interviews advance the ground for toolmaking

An interview begins from what is already held, tests it against another person's experience and leaves changed ground for what follows. The interviews are successive movements of comprehension, not independent accounts collected for synthesis at the end.

Present work is the way in. A concrete occurrence shows what an abstract description hides: the goal being served, where responsibility stands, where judgment enters, what must be understood, where the work passes and what shows it is complete. That ground lets the tool emerge without the present arrangement becoming its design.

These are directions for comprehension, not a questionnaire. Ask upward from procedure toward the goal and inward from difficulty toward judgment. Digest what changed before deciding what the next encounter most needs.
